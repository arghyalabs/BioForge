# BioForge Scientific Principles

## 1. Core Philosophy

Traditional programming asks, *"What computation should the computer perform?"* This leads to code optimized for the machine rather than the science. 

**BioLang**, the native language of the BioForge platform, asks a fundamentally different set of questions:
* *"What biological system am I describing?"*
* *"What mechanisms govern this system?"*
* *"What physical laws influence its behavior?"*
* *"What happens when that system evolves through time?"*

By reorienting the language around the biology rather than the machine, BioForge ensures that models are explicit, assumptions are visible, and the simulation remains deeply tied to scientific reality rather than computational convenience.

---

## 2. The 15 Non-Negotiable Scientific Principles

1. **Scientific correctness is more important than visual appearance.** 
   A visually stunning animation of a protein that violates thermodynamics is a cartoon, not a simulation. Visualizations must strictly reflect the underlying mathematical and physical state.
2. **Simulation state is the source of truth.**
   The 3D/4D renderer or measurement block merely observes the `SimulationState`. No physics, forces, or interactions are ever computed in the visualization layer.
3. **Units are first-class.**
   A number without a unit is scientifically meaningless. Units are deeply integrated into the language to prevent dimensional errors and enforce physical consistency.
4. **Models must expose assumptions.**
   No model is perfect. The assumptions inherent in a given biological or mathematical model must be explicitly declared and visible to the user.
5. **Approximations must be visible.**
   When continuous space is discretized or infinite series are truncated, these approximations must be documented and accessible, never hidden as "magic numbers."
6. **Validation must be explicit.**
   A model is only as good as its validation. Every model must reference how and against what experimental or analytical data it was validated.
7. **Uncertainty must be represented where possible.**
   Biological parameters often come with error bars. The simulation pipeline should acknowledge and propagate parameter uncertainty where feasible.
8. **Experimental data must remain distinguishable from simulation results.**
   The provenance of data—whether observed in a lab or generated *in silico*—must never be blurred.
9. **Predictions must not be presented as established biological facts.**
   When a simulation explores unmapped territory, the resulting outputs are *hypotheses* or *predictions*, not truths.
10. **Reproducibility must be supported.**
    An experiment run today must yield statistically identical insights tomorrow. The platform must capture the entire state required to reproduce a run.
11. **Scientific provenance must be preserved.**
    Every interaction parameter, rate constant, and physical quantity must trace back to a verifiable source (publication, database, theoretical derivation).
12. **Different biological scales may require different mathematical models.**
    You cannot simulate tissue mechanics with quantum chromodynamics. The platform embraces scale-appropriate modeling.
13. **Do not pretend one solver can accurately simulate all of biology.**
    A solver optimized for stochastic gene expression is distinct from one integrating Newtonian equations of motion. Solvers are domain-specific.
14. **Do not claim exact replication of biological reality.**
    All models are abstractions. We claim rigorous, useful approximations of reality, not omniscient replication.
15. **Aim for progressively increasing fidelity.**
    Start with a simple model (e.g., rigid body). Progress to flexible models, then polarizable models. Fidelity is a dial to be turned, not a binary state.

## 3. The Reality → Model → Simulation → Validation Loop

Science in BioForge is an iterative process:

1. **Real biology**: The messy, complex ground truth.
2. **Experimental observations**: Empirical data gathered from the real world.
3. **Scientific model**: Conceptual abstraction of the observations.
4. **Computational model**: The translation of the scientific model into BioLang.
5. **Simulation**: Execution of the model through time using solvers.
6. **Prediction**: Novel outputs generated by the simulation.
7. **Experimental comparison**: Matching predictions against new or withheld data.
8. **Validation / Model refinement**: Quantifying error and adjusting the model.
9. **Improved model**: The cycle repeats.

---

## 4. Simulation Fidelity Dimensions

Simulations are not simply "accurate" or "inaccurate." They vary across multiple axes of fidelity:

* **Structural fidelity**: How accurately is the physical geometry represented? (e.g., coarse-grained vs. all-atom)
* **Physical fidelity**: Are the fundamental laws (thermodynamics, electromagnetism) rigorously applied?
* **Chemical fidelity**: Are bond breaking, formation, and quantum effects supported?
* **Biological fidelity**: Does the model capture high-level biological behaviors (e.g., transcription, cell division)?
* **Spatial fidelity**: What is the resolution of space? (Continuous vs. discrete lattice)
* **Temporal fidelity**: What is the time-step resolution? (Femtoseconds vs. hours)
* **Experimental validation level**: How rigorously has the model been tested against empirical data?
* **Parameter confidence**: Are the parameters guessed, fitted, or directly measured?

## 5. Model Documentation Template

Every scientific model integrated into BioForge must include a manifest or documentation block adhering to the following structure:

```text
Model: [Name and brief description]
Numerical method: [Integrator/solver type, e.g., Velocity Verlet]
Spatial resolution: [e.g., All-atom, coarse-grained, continuum]
Temporal resolution: [e.g., 2 fs time step]
Parameters: [Force field or parameter set used]
Units: [Base unit system utilized internally]
Data sources: [Citations for parameters and structures]
Assumptions: [e.g., Implicit solvent, rigid bonds]
Approximations: [e.g., Cutoff radius for electrostatics]
Experimental reference: [Which empirical data bounds this model]
Validation: [Summary of validation tests passed]
Uncertainty: [Known error bounds or confidence intervals]
Known limitations: [Where this model breaks down]
```

## 6. Distinction Hierarchy

To maintain scientific integrity, BioForge strictly enforces a hierarchy of confidence. These concepts must **NEVER** be conflated:

1. **REAL-WORLD OBSERVATION** *(Highest confidence)*: Raw empirical data from physical experiments.
2. **SCIENTIFIC MODEL**: Theoretical frameworks derived from observations.
3. **COMPUTATIONAL MODEL**: BioLang implementation of the scientific model.
4. **SIMULATION RESULT**: The raw data trajectory produced by executing the computational model.
5. **PREDICTION** *(Lowest confidence, must be tested)*: Extrapolations beyond validated regimes.

---

## 7. Unit System Principles

* **All physical quantities carry units.** No bare floats are allowed for physical properties.
* **Dimensional analysis** catches errors at compile-time and runtime.
* **Internal representation**: Uses the AKMA (Ångström, Kilocalorie/mole, Mass, AKMA time) or natural Real units for maximum floating-point precision and numerical stability.
* **Language-level mapping**: User-facing code uses standard scientific units (nm, ps, mV, mM) with zero-cost, safe conversion.
* **Invalid operations** (e.g., adding a distance to a temperature) trigger immediate compilation errors.

---

## 8. Solver Selection Principles

Different domains require radically different mathematical approaches. BioForge does not use a "one-size-fits-all" engine:

* **Molecular Dynamics (MD)**: For atomic/molecular scale (Newtonian equations of motion).
* **Brownian Dynamics**: For mesoscale and implicit solvent regimes.
* **Ordinary Differential Equations (ODE)**: For bulk reaction kinetics and pathways.
* **Partial Differential Equations (PDE)**: For diffusion and gradient transport.
* **Stochastic Simulation Algorithms (SSA)**: For single-molecule and low-copy-number kinetics.
* **Agent-based Modeling**: For cell populations and tissue mechanics.
* **Finite Element Method (FEM)**: For continuum mechanics and structural biology at macro scales.

---

## 9. Scientific Provenance

Every parameter—whether it is a van der Waals radius, a spring constant, or a transcription rate—must explicitly trace its origin. The platform tracks whether a parameter originated from a peer-reviewed publication, a curated database, a computational derivation, or a user-defined estimate.

---

## 10. Reproducibility Requirements

BioForge guarantees that any execution can be precisely reproduced. The platform captures the **full experiment state**, which includes:
* BioLang compiler version
* Model version and parameter sets
* Solver engine version and numerical settings
* Random number generator seeds
* Hardware/Architecture signatures (where floating-point math diverges)

---

## 11. Scientific Accuracy vs Visual Beauty

The BioForge pipeline is strictly linear and unidirectional:
**Scientific Model → Mathematical Model → Numerical Solver → SimulationState → Renderer**

The renderer is purely an observer. The platform rejects the paradigm of "animation masquerading as physics." If an entity moves in the renderer, it is strictly because a force was calculated in the solver and integrated into the `SimulationState`. Visual beauty is a byproduct of scientific accuracy, never a substitute for it.
